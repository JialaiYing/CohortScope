Run of show

Answer to “can I make it a working product?” — No

Not a time problem. Five pre-registered tests say the signal isn't there, so “making it work” means retuning thresholds or cherry-picking — which destroys the only thing here that can't be copied. You already have a working product: the adequacy checker. It runs offline in half a second and answers a question a curator actually has. Present that.

Before you present — 40 minutes, in this order
10 minOpen everything now, in this order, and don't touch it again. Terminal at the repo root · the dossier page in a browser tab · a second tab on the sweep chart section
5 minDry-run both commands once. Confirm they print. If the terminal font is small, make it big now. Judges will squint otherwise.
20 minRehearse out loud twice with a timer. Not in your head — out loud. First run will be long. The second is the real one.
5 minMemorise only the first sentence and the last sentence. Improvise the middle. Everything else you can read off this page.
The pitch
0:00

Open on the finding, not the failure

25s
DOStand still. Nothing on screen yet, or the dossier title.
Say

“Museums publish gigapixel scans of their paintings. Almost every AI analysis of those paintings throws that resolution away at download time. We measured what's actually left, and it isn't a brushstroke — not on one painting out of a hundred and eight.”

0:25

Say what you built and take the turn

35s
Say

“We set out to flag which Rijksmuseum ‘Rembrandts’ look anomalous against firmly attributed ones. It does not work. So I'm going to show you two things: how we know that to a standard most projects won't have — and the thing we found along the way that does work.”

Say “it does not work” flatly and keep going. Do not soften it, do not apologise. The confidence is the pitch.

1:00

Live demo — the thing that works

60s
RUNOffline, no network, finishes in about half a second.
python tiles.py --plan
Say — while it prints

“A hundred and eight paintings. Sixty-four can be answered at brushstroke resolution from what the museum publishes. Forty-four cannot — and every one comes with a stated reason, not a silent drop.”

“The Night Watch is in the second group. Its published imagery is 0.31 mm per pixel and you need 0.20. That is a real answer to give a curator: we cannot answer this from your images, here is the number, and here is what you'd have to re-shoot.”

THENSwitch to the dossier tab, scroll to the per-work table, type night in the search box.

This beat is your product demo. Give it the full minute — it is the only part a judge can imagine someone using tomorrow.

2:00

The proof nobody else will have

50s
RUNBack to the terminal. Let them read the pattern in the subject lines.
git log --oneline -12
Say

“Every experiment in this repo has a design document committed before the data existed. Read the log: pre-register, then result. Pre-register, then result. Five times.”

“The thresholds, the random seed, the decision rule — all transcribed from those documents and never edited to move an outcome. When a locked rule cost us samples, we recorded the loss instead of changing the rule. You can check that with timestamps.”

If one judge leans in, this is the beat they lean in on. It is the part that cannot be reproduced after the fact by anyone who didn't do it.

2:50

The result, fast

40s
SHOWDossier tab → the sweep chart, then scroll one section to the confound table.
Say

“Five held-out tests, all fail. But each one killed a specific excuse: too few samples, then the wrong scale, then the features, then the network never saw brushwork, then — maybe we just picked the wrong resolution.”

“So we swept it. Eight points, all at chance. And in all four tests, a single column of camera metadata separates the paintings better than our entire pipeline. What the model was really learning was how the photograph was taken.”

3:30

Close

30s
Say — memorise this one

“The model is ordinary. Anyone here could fit a ResNet against a centroid. What isn't ordinary is that we can prove what we predicted before we saw the answer, and that we shipped the negative result instead of tuning until something looked good.”

“And the useful thing survived it: tell me your collection, and I'll tell you which questions your imagery can actually answer. That part works today.”

THENStop talking. Do not add a summary. Let the silence sit.
Questions you will get

“So… it failed?”

“The method failed. The experiment worked.” A result you can trust that says no is worth more than a demo you can't check that says yes. Then pivot straight to the adequacy checker.

“Why not just use a better model — a transformer, a fine-tune?”

“Might help. But we'd be fitting a bigger model to the same inadequate pixels, and we now know exactly how inadequate they are. Fix the input before you upgrade the model — that's the whole finding.”

“Isn't this just data cleaning?”

“It's a measurement error that every downstream number inherits. Our texture features were comparing 0.1 mm on one painting to 3.5 mm on another and calling it the same feature. That's not cleaning, that's the experiment being invalid.”

“How do we know you didn't just tune until it failed / succeeded?”

“Check the git log — design documents are committed before the data. And for the sweep we fixed the multiple-comparison correction in advance, for all eight tests, before computing any of them.”

“What would you do with more time?”

“Not another model. Better imagery — and we can name exactly which 23 paintings would need re-shooting and at what resolution. Nine works in the whole collection beat 0.05 mm per pixel. The data to test a finer hypothesis doesn't exist yet.”

“Who would use this?”

“A collection holder planning a digitisation budget, and any research group about to point a model at museum images. Right now they'd find out after the analysis. This tells them before.”

Do not, under any circumstances
  • Apologise, or say “unfortunately”. You have a result. Deliver it like one.
  • Promise it would work with more time. You tested that. It doesn't.
  • Run anything that needs the network. Venue wifi will betray you. Both demo commands are offline.
  • Re-run the pipeline live. Tiling takes 85 minutes. Show the finished artifacts.
  • Read the long explanations aloud. Judges read faster than you talk. Point, then say the one line.
If the laptop dies

The whole pitch works with no screen at all: 108 paintings, 64 answerable, 44 not, including the Night Watch. Five pre-registered tests, all negative. A camera-metadata column beat our model four times out of four. Those five numbers are the pitch.
